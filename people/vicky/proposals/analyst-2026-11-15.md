## Topic 1 (recommended first): People ahead, companies behind

- **The question.** In 2025 people in Portugal used generative AI more than the EU average, but Portuguese companies used AI far less. Why the gap, and what does each number measure?
- **Checked figures (Eurostat, 2025).** People 16 to 74 using generative AI tools in the last three months: **Portugal 38.7%, EU 32.7%** (13th of 27). For work: **19.9% against 15.4%**. Ages 16 to 24: **76.5% against 63.8%**; 55 to 74: **12.1% against 12.0%**. Companies with 10+ staff using any AI technology: **Portugal 11.5%, EU 20.0%** (21st of 27).
- **The AI path.** AI directly, 0 steps. It links to Humans and AI (people using AI at work while their employers have not adopted it). Places: Portugal and Europe. The Algarve only if INE's own release breaks the people's figure down by NUTS II region (to check at INE before a draft).
- **Primary sources.** Eurostat isoc_ai_iaiu (individuals) and isoc_eb_ai (enterprises); INE's 2025 ICT surveys of households and of companies for the national release and any regional table.
- **What the data can show.** Two official, comparable 2025 snapshots, the EU comparison, the age split, and that one in five people in Portugal used generative AI for work while one company in nine used AI.
- **What it cannot show.** A trend for people: the individuals' question exists only for 2025. It cannot show that the workers who use AI do so with their employer's tools; the two surveys count different units (people against companies of 10+ staff, finance excluded), so the two percentages are not on one scale and the piece never subtracts one from the other. It cannot show how often or how well AI is used.
- **Rough size.** 700 to 900 words. Chart: two paired bars (Portugal, EU) for "people using generative AI" and "companies using AI", each panel with its own label and source, not one shared axis.

## Topic 2: One firm in nine, and the gap that opened

- **The question.** Portugal's share of companies using AI was close to the EU's in 2021. By 2025 the gap was more than eight points. Where did it open?
- **Checked figures (Eurostat isoc_eb_ai, 10+ staff, % of companies).** Portugal 7.2 (2021), 7.9 (2023), 8.6 (2024), 11.5 (2025). EU 7.7, 8.1, 13.5, 20.0. The gap: 0.5, 0.2, 4.9, 8.4 points. By size in 2025, Portugal against EU: 10 to 49 staff 9.4 against 17.0; 50 to 249 staff 18.2 against 30.4; 250+ staff 49.2 against 55.0.
- **The AI path.** AI directly, 0 steps. Places: Portugal and Europe.
- **Primary sources.** Eurostat isoc_eb_ai and its metadata; INE's 2025 ICT survey of companies.
- **What the data can show.** The EU sped up from 2024; Portugal grew more slowly. The gap is widest for small and medium firms; large Portuguese firms are close to the EU.
- **What it cannot show, and the comparisons to judge.** There is no 2022 point. Whether 2021 compares with 2023 onwards (the list of AI technologies asked about may have changed) is to be checked in Eurostat's metadata before a draft; until then the main line is 2023 to 2025. Firms under 10 staff and finance are left out. It cannot show depth of use, or effects on jobs.
- **Rough size.** 600 to 800 words. Chart: two lines (Portugal, EU), 2023 to 2025 (2021 marked separately if comparable), plus the 2025 size split as paired bars.
- **Note.** Topics 1 and 2 share the company figures. If the curator wants one piece, topic 1 carries topic 2's size split as its second chart.

## Topic 3: Sixty megawatts and a 1,000 MW goal

- **The question.** The Government says Portugal had about 60 MW of computing capacity in 2025 and needs 700 to 1,000 MW by 2030 to meet AI demand. How far is that, and how does one private campus in Sines compare?
- **The AI path.** AI directly for the national target (the AI Agenda sets it for AI demand); two steps for Sines (AI runs in data centres, which draw on the grid). Places: Sines (Alentejo), Portugal, Europe.
- **Primary sources.** RCM 2/2026 (National AI Agenda) and RCM 70/2026 (National Data Centre Plan), official texts. Start Campus's own release (August 2024) for Sines, as a company claim. REN for grid capacity, if it publishes one.
- **What the data can show.** The size of the jump the Government plans (12 to 17 times in five years by its own figures), the gap it states between demand (about 170 MW) and capacity (about 60 MW) in 2025, and the plan's own words that installed capacity "practically doubled" from 2020 to 2025 while staying "significantly below" the EU average, with "a low rate" of projects carried out.
- **What it cannot show.** How much of any capacity runs AI rather than other cloud work. Whether "computing capacity" in the Agenda and "IT capacity" in Start Campus's claim measure the same thing (I check before comparing them; if not, they go side by side and are never added). Announced capacity is not built capacity. The scenarios are Government targets, not forecasts from an independent body.
- **Rough size.** 800 to 1,000 words. Chart: four bars in MW (2025 capacity about 60; 2025 demand about 170; 2030 targets about 700 and about 1,000), all from RCM 2/2026; Sines's 1.2 GW claim in a labelled note, not on the same axis.

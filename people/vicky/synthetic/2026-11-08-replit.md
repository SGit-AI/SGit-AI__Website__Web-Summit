# Synthetic reader test: Replit's company page, three personas, judged on reader value

By synthetic@web-summit.vicky, for Vicky Wang. Findings only: no page was changed. First written on 8 November 2026 as a usability test; revised the same day at Vicky's request to centre on what each reader gets from the page. The browser evidence is unchanged. The conductor's triage of the original version is in `2026-11-08-replit-triage.md` and is not altered here.

## 1. Summary and disclosures

- **Tested:** https://promptugal.news/companies/replit/ and, from it, /articles/five-ai-builders-to-know.html and /my-day/.
- **Site version:** v0.1.715, in the page footer and in the page's `index.md`. **Test time:** 8 November 2026, 15:19 to 15:21 UTC (the same clock time in Lisbon).
- **How:** Playwright with the installed Chromium; six fresh contexts (three personas, each on phone 390 x 844 with touch and on desktop 1440 x 900), each arriving directly on the page.
- **I already know this page.** I read its `index.md` and the page's structure before the personas were defined, and I wrote this revision with the evidence in front of me. So nothing below is a first impression. The "before reading" expectations, the "what I now understand" summaries and the recommendations are my imagined version of each reader, built from the evidence. They are hypotheses, not user behaviour, and the three personas are one agent, not three independent readers.
- **Labels used:** OBSERVED means I checked it in the browser. SIMULATED means an imagined reader's reaction. RECOMMENDATION means my suggestion, which is not a fact about the page.
- **Limitations:** navigation was scripted (I scrolled and located text; I did not discover routes the way a person would). I looked at some screenshots by eye (the first screens, the session lists, the phone newsroom section) and relied on measurements for the rest. Not tested: the phone's collapsed "On this page" list, the graph, the Ask button, the vault, other browsers. No time zone appears on the page for any session, and I have not added one.

## 3. Persona A: non-technical founder

*Goal given:* has not used Replit; wants to understand whether it could help build a product prototype and what technical knowledge is needed.

### 3.1 Before reading (SIMULATED)
I came because someone mentioned Replit as a way to get a product built without hiring developers. I wanted to know: what is it, can it produce something I could show people, do I need to code, roughly what it costs. I expected a company profile with a clear one-liner and perhaps some mention of who uses it.

### 3.2 What I actually found (OBSERVED)
- **First screen, phone:** site bar, title "Replit", the line "Based in United States; at Web Summit Lisbon 2026 as a partner; speaker's company. Public facts only, each with its source.", a collapsed "On this page +" bar, then a graph of connections. The first screen ends partway into the "What they do" row.
- **"What they do"** (facts table) starts 675 px down (0.8 screens): "Agentic software creation platform that lets anyone build applications in natural language; millions of users and 500,000+ business users."
- **"About Replit"** (`#company-about`) starts 1091 px down (1.3 screens): the company calls itself "the AI platform that helps you turn your ideas into real outcomes"; "You describe the app or website you want in plain language, and Replit builds it, with no coding needed, the company says on Replit, "AI App and Website Builder", read 8 November 2026"; "Its Replit Agent writes, tests, fixes and publishes the software, and searches the web when it needs fresh information". Each sentence names its source and read date.
- **Flows section** (`#flow-build-through-chat`, about 3.8 screens down): "like having an engineering team on demand, with no coding experience required"; the person's part is "to describe and to give feedback"; the agent connects a database, sign-in and services such as Stripe and OpenAI, tests the app in a browser and fixes what it finds, and can build other agents and automations.
- **Not on the page:** the words "prototype" and "MVP" do not appear. A search for price or cost finds only the site's own reading-credit widget, not Replit's pricing. The facts table has a link to replit.com.
- **Phone vs desktop:** on the phone the explanation is partly visible on the first screen but sits below a graph. On desktop the first screen has the title, the one-line tagline, the full "On this page" list and the graph; "What they do" starts at 913 px, just under one screen (the window is 900 px), and "About Replit" at 1274 px (1.4 screens).

### 3.3 Did I get what I needed? Partly (SIMULATED)
**Answered:** what Replit is (a tool where you describe an app and it builds it), and that no coding is needed, as the company's own claim, clearly marked as the company's words. **Unclear:** whether what it builds is good enough to be a prototype I could show to others, what it costs, and what I would still have to decide myself. The page never claims to answer these, so this is a gap in my goal, not an error in the page.

### 3.4 What I now understand (SIMULATED, in my own words)
"Replit is a service where you tell an AI assistant what app or website you want, in ordinary language, and it does the building, testing and fixing, and puts it online. It says it is for anyone, including people who cannot code, and it says lots of people already use it, including businesses." 
**Not established by the page:** how good the results are; whether a prototype would hold up with real users; what it costs; what I would need to supply beyond a clear description; any evidence beyond the company's and Web Summit's own statements. The user figures ("millions", "500,000+") have no source next to them in the table row.

### 3.5 Nice to have
- *Missing essentials for this goal:* none that the page promises. The page is a news profile and does not claim to advise on building a product.
- *Optional improvements (SIMULATED wishes, not defects):* the plain "you describe it and it builds it" sentence near the title, before the graph on the phone; a note on where to check price and limits (the replit.com link is already there). Vicky decided on 8 November not to add pricing, product limits or extra sections to this page for now, so the second is recorded as a reader wish, not a recommendation to act.

### 3.6 Would I recommend this page? Conditionally (RECOMMENDATION, SIMULATED)
Yes, to a curious non-technical person who wants a quick, sourced idea of what Replit is and who is behind it, because the claims are attributed and dated. No, to someone deciding whether to build their product with it: nothing on the page helps with that decision. For that reader, the page would need to say plainly that it does not cover fit, cost or limits, or point to where they are covered.

## 4. Persona B: developer

*Goal given:* knows AI coding tools but has not read this page; wants to choose a relevant Replit summit session, when and where it takes place, and whether they can attend.

### 4.1 Before reading (SIMULATED)
I came because I am going to Web Summit and want to catch something from Replit. I wanted a session that is useful to someone who already uses AI coding tools, the day, time and stage, and whether I can walk in or need something extra. I expected a short list of sessions with a line on what each covers, and a clear flag on any that are restricted.

### 4.2 What I actually found (OBSERVED)
- **Sessions** (`#summit`, 1641 px down on the phone, 1.9 screens; the three entries take about 0.9 screens):
  1. "14:00 Making useful intelligence accessible to everyone. Tue 10 Nov, until 14:20 · Centre Stage · Michele Catasta speaks"
  2. "15:00 Press conference with Replit President Michele Catasta. Tue 10 Nov, until 15:20 · Stage 2 · Michele Catasta speaks ... media only", plus a "MEDIA ONLY" badge
  3. "16:00 What does a self-driving company look like? Tue 10 Nov, until 16:20 · Stage 13 · Michele Catasta speaks", plus an "OUR PICK" badge
- Each entry carries a long source in brackets and a "read 2026-11-08 by programme@web-summit.vicky" note. None has a description of its content or format.
- **Restrictions:** only the press conference has one. Nothing explains "media only" or "Our pick". A search for accreditation, press pass, ticket or registration finds nothing beyond the press conference line. No time zone is shown for any session.
- **Technical content a developer might use:** the flows section says the agent builds the app, connects a database, sign-in and services, tests it in a browser in a "reflection loop", can use "Extended Thinking and high-power models", and builds other agents. The sources list quotes a Temporal page: "Replit migrated their popular coding agent to Temporal".
- **Phone vs desktop:** on the phone the sessions are one column, with the graph above showing the same three times but cut-off titles. On desktop, "At the summit", "People" and "Related companies" sit side by side from 1646 px (1.8 screens), with the sessions in the left column.
- "Add to my day" is covered in section 7.

### 4.3 Did I get what I needed? Partly (SIMULATED)
**Answered:** the day, start and end time and stage for each of three sessions, and that the 15:00 press conference is media only. **Unclear:** which session is technical enough for me (titles are broad; no descriptions); what "Our pick" is based on; whether the two non-press sessions are open to any attendee or need a particular pass. I would have to go to Web Summit's own page for these.

### 4.4 What I now understand (SIMULATED, in my own words)
"Replit makes an AI agent that takes a description and produces a working app, tests it and fixes its own bugs, and can wire in a database and outside services. It is aimed at people who want software built from a description, including people who do not code. On Tuesday 10 November its president, Michele Catasta, appears three times: a talk at 14:00 on Centre Stage, a press conference at 15:00 that is for journalists, and a talk at 16:00 on Stage 13."
**Not established by the page:** what the talks will actually cover; which language, tooling or workflow details matter to a developer (for instance whether you can see and edit the code the agent writes); whether regular attendees can enter the 14:00 and 16:00 sessions; anything about how Replit compares with other tools.

### 4.5 Nice to have
- *Missing essentials for this goal:* a clear statement of who can attend the non-press sessions, or of where to check; a plain meaning of "media only" for someone who is not press.
- *Optional improvements:* one line per session on what it covers; an explanation of "Our pick"; shorter source text on the phone so time, stage and badge stand out (each entry's source takes several lines); a taller "Add to my day" button on the phone.

### 4.6 Would I recommend this page? Conditionally (RECOMMENDATION, SIMULATED)
Yes, to another attendee who wants a fast, accurate list of when and where Replit's Tuesday sessions are, because the date, time and stage are consistent and sourced. No, to someone who wants to decide which session is worth their time or whether they will be let in: the page does not help with either. It would need session descriptions and a line on attendance conditions.

## 5. Persona C: technology journalist

*Goal given:* has basic awareness of Replit; wants useful company background and relevant published reporting.

### 5.1 Before reading (SIMULATED)
I came from a mention of Web Summit's AI speakers and wanted a fast, reliable picture of Replit: what it sells, who runs it, what is claimed about it, and what has been written already so I do not repeat it. I expected a profile with sources I can check, and links to coverage, including independent coverage.

### 5.2 What I actually found (OBSERVED)
- **Background:** "About Replit" is four sentences, each followed by a named source and "read 8 November 2026". The first three rest on Replit's own pages ("AI Coding Agent: Build Apps Through Chat", "AI App and Website Builder"); the fourth rests on two Web Summit pages ("Meet our global partners", "Replit"). The facts table adds: based in United States; "At Web Summit as: a partner; speaker's company"; "Confirmed for 2026: Yes: listed for LIS26 on Web Summit's event partners page". "Where this comes from" (`#sources`, 4638 px on the phone) lists six sources, including Lusa on the speaker and a Temporal page about Replit's coding agent. The users figure in the table has no source beside it. The page says "We have not asked Replit yet" under "Our question for them". (Wording for the "At Web Summit as" and "Confirmed for 2026" rows, the source list contents and the "not asked" line is from the page's `index.md`; in the browser I confirmed the section headings and their positions and the rendered "What they do" row, not each of these lines.)
- **Related reporting:** "From the newsroom" (`#newsroom`, 2823 px, 3.3 screens down on the phone; 2388 px, 2.7 screens on desktop) holds one entry: "Five AI builders on Web Summit's speaker list worth knowing, 8 November 2026", linking to `../../articles/five-ai-builders-to-know.html`. No summary or mention of Replit under it. The link is dark, bold and not underlined; the page's other links are green and underlined. Clicking or tapping it opened the article.
- **The article** (903 words): its summary says "Five worth knowing, from Replit, Manus AI, MiniMax, ModelBest and You.com". Replit is named first and nine times; the Replit part is one short paragraph under "Building the apps", repeating the "no coding needed" claim and mentioning the press conference. It states what it does not claim, including "This is not a ranking".
- **Where article and page differ:** the article says "The only session with a time on the pages we read is Replit's press conference, which is for media only." The company page lists two other timed sessions (14:00, 16:00). The article places the press conference "on Stage 2 in the Media Village"; the page says "Stage 2". I did not check whether these name the same place.
- **Phone vs desktop:** the phone starts "On this page" collapsed; on desktop the list, with "From the newsroom" by name, is visible on the first screen. Link target and wording are the same on both.

### 5.3 Did I get what I needed? Partly (SIMULATED)
**Answered:** a short, sourced description of what Replit does and its Web Summit status; one relevant published article, found and opened. **Unclear:** what the article is before I click it (the entry gives only a title); whether there is any coverage beyond one roundup; any independent view of the company, since most of the background is Replit's own description; where the user figures come from. The mismatch on sessions between the article and the page would make me trust one of them less until checked.

### 5.4 What I now understand (SIMULATED, in my own words)
"Replit sells a platform where an AI agent builds apps from a description, which it presents as needing no coding. It is an event partner at Web Summit and its president, Michele Catasta, speaks and holds a press conference on Tuesday 10 November. This newsroom has run one piece naming Replit, a roundup of five AI speakers, and has not yet put questions to the company."
**Not established by the page:** any independent assessment of Replit's claims or numbers; Replit's history, leadership beyond the one named person, funding or competitors; whether anything else has been published about it.

### 5.5 Nice to have
- *Missing essentials for this goal:* a line under the newsroom link saying what the article is and that Replit is one of five; agreement between the article and the page on sessions (or a note saying which is current). Both are observed gaps, set out in section 7.
- *Optional improvements:* the link styled like other links; an inline source for the users figure; a statement on whether independent coverage exists.

### 5.6 Would I recommend this page? Conditionally (RECOMMENDATION, SIMULATED)
Yes, to a colleague who needs a quick, transparent starting point on Replit and Web Summit, because every sentence is sourced and dated and the page admits what it has not yet asked. No, as a substitute for independent reporting: the background is largely the company's own account and the one linked article is a roundup. It would need the entry described, the article reconciled with the page, and ideally independent sources.

## 6. Comparison across personas

- **Served well:** the journalist and the event-going developer, on the questions the page is built for: who and what the company is, with sources, and when and where its sessions are. Both can find what they came for within roughly two screens (sessions, About) to three and a half (the newsroom link) on the phone, and within about three on desktop.
- **Served less well:** the founder, whose real question (is this right for my product) is outside what a news profile offers; they get a clear explanation of what Replit is but no help deciding. The developer is also under-served on the choice between sessions and on attendance.
- **Most valuable improvements, in order of reader value (RECOMMENDATIONS):**
  1. Explain "media only", and say who can attend the other sessions or where to check (developer; also anyone not press).
  2. Make the newsroom entry say what it is, and make the article and the page agree on sessions (journalist).
  3. A short plain explanation of what Replit does close to the title (founder; helps everyone, most on the phone).
  4. One line on what each session covers (developer).
- **Phone vs desktop:** the same conclusions hold. The differences are position (explanation partly on the first phone screen but below a graph; just under the first screen on desktop), the collapsed contents list on the phone, and the sessions in one long column on the phone versus three columns on desktop.

## 8. What worked well (OBSERVED)

- Every sentence of the About text names its source and read date, and the page states what it has not yet asked ("We have not asked Replit yet").
- Date, start, end and stage are given for every session in a consistent form; the press conference is marked media only twice (text and badge); no time zone was invented.
- Add to my day works in a fresh browser and says plainly where picks are kept.
- The newsroom link leads to a real article that openly states what it does not claim.

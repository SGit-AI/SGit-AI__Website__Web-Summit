# Synthetic reader test: Replit's company page, three personas

By synthetic@web-summit.vicky, for Vicky Wang. Findings only: no page was changed.

## 1. Summary

- **Tested:** https://promptugal.news/companies/replit/ (and, from it, /articles/five-ai-builders-to-know.html and /my-day/).
- **Site version:** v0.1.715, shown in the page footer (text at the end of the page, about 9.4 screens down on the phone) and in the page's `index.md`.
- **Test time:** 8 November 2026, 15:19 to 15:21 UTC (Lisbon was on UTC+0 that day, so the same clock time). The site header showed "Sun 8 Nov".
- **How:** Playwright with the installed Chromium. Six fresh browser contexts, one per persona per device, each arriving directly on the page with no earlier navigation. Phone: 390 x 844, mobile and touch (taps). Desktop: 1440 x 900 (clicks).
- **What these are:** simulations by one agent. The three personas are not independent users, and nothing here is user feedback, timing or statistics. I had read the page's `index.md` before the personas were defined, so a persona's "not knowing" is imagined, not real.
- **Limitations:**
  - Navigation was scripted. I gave each persona a goal and then scrolled the page and located text; I did not discover a route the way a person would, and I cannot show what a person would tap first.
  - I inspected these screenshots by eye: both first screens on the founder runs, the phone and desktop session lists, and the phone "From the newsroom" section. Other layout points rest on measurements only (scroll width, element positions and sizes).
  - Not tested: the collapsed "On this page" list on the phone, the graph widget, the Ask button, the third "Add to my day" button, removing a pick, the vault, and any browser other than Chromium.
  - My first script run failed because of a flaw in my own script (a changing button selector). I fixed the script and re-ran everything; only the final runs are reported.
  - No time zone is shown for any session on the page, and I have not added one.

## 2. Results at a glance

| Persona and goal | Phone | Desktop | Top suggested fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Non-technical founder: could Replit help build a prototype, and what skill is needed | partial | partial | Say in the plain first lines what kind of things Replit builds, and point to Replit's own page for limits and cost |
| Developer: pick a session, when and where, and can they attend | partial | partial | Explain "Media only" (who it is for) and carry the marker through to the My day list; add a line on what each session covers |
| Technology journalist: company background and relevant published reporting | partial | partial | Under "From the newsroom", add one line saying what the article is (a roundup of five) and fix its link styling; reconcile the article with the page on sessions |

How the earlier three tasks map: understanding the company is the founder (and the About text), finding a session is the developer, finding related reporting is the journalist. The plain "what does Replit do" question passes on both devices. Each persona is partial because their fuller goal needs something the page does not give, as set out below.

## 3. Persona A: non-technical founder

**Goal given:** has not used Replit; wants to understand whether it could help build a product prototype and what technical knowledge is needed. No section names or route were given.

### Phone (fresh context, 390 x 844)

**OBSERVED, navigation and evidence**
- Arrived on the page. The first screen (0 to 844 px) holds the site bar, the tagline "People and AI agents, reporting together.", the title "Replit", the line "Based in United States; at Web Summit Lisbon 2026 as a partner; speaker's company. Public facts only, each with its source.", a collapsed "On this page +" bar, and a graph of Replit's connections. The first screen ends partway through the "What they do" row.
- "What they do" (a row in the facts table) starts at 675 px, about 0.8 screens down: "Agentic software creation platform that lets anyone build applications in natural language; millions of users and 500,000+ business users."
- "About Replit" (anchor `#company-about`) starts at 1091 px, about 1.3 screens down. It says: "You describe the app or website you want in plain language, and Replit builds it, with no coding needed, the company says on Replit, "AI App and Website Builder", read 8 November 2026." and "Its Replit Agent writes, tests, fixes and publishes the software".
- A further quote, "like having an engineering team on demand, with no coding experience required", sits in the flows section (`#flow-build-through-chat`) at about 3.8 screens down.
- The words "prototype" and "MVP" do not appear on the page. A search for price or cost finds only the site's own reading-credit widget ("It sets the price: not useful is free, excellent is double"), not anything about Replit's pricing.
- No horizontal overflow (scroll width 390 = viewport 390).

**Task result: partial.** Criterion: the reader can say what Replit does and whether it needs coding skill, and can judge fit for a prototype. The first two are answered, in the company's own words and marked as such. Fit for a prototype is left to the reader to infer.

**SIMULATED interpretation (a hypothesis, not user behaviour):** This founder would probably take away "describe an app in words and it builds it, no coding needed". They might stumble on "Agentic" and "natural language" in the table row, and might not know what "business users" means. After reading, they may still ask whether the result is good enough to show investors, what it costs, and what happens when something breaks. None of that is promised on the page, so the doubt is reasonable, but the page is a news profile and may not be meant to answer it.

**Suggested fixes (suggestions only)**
- P3: put the plain sentence ("You describe the app or website you want in plain language, and Replit builds it") first, or make the table row say it in everyday words. On the phone the graph comes before any explanation of what the company does.
- P3: add a pointer to Replit's own pages for pricing and limits (the table already links replit.com), so a founder knows where to look next.

### Desktop (fresh context, 1440 x 900)

**OBSERVED**
- First screen: the title, the same "Based in United States..." line, the "On this page" list with all section names visible (About Replit, At the summit, People, Related companies, From the newsroom, and so on) and the graph. No sentence about what Replit does is on the first screen.
- "What they do" starts at 913 px, just under one screen (the viewport is 900 px high). "About Replit" starts at 1274 px (1.4 screens). The flows quote is at about 3.1 screens.
- No horizontal overflow.

**Task result: partial.** Same criterion and same gap as on the phone, and the answer is one scroll further down than on the phone.

**SIMULATED interpretation:** A founder on a large screen sees a graph of company names first and may read it as the page's main message. They would need to scroll to learn what the company does. The visible "On this page" list would help them jump to "About Replit" if they notice it.

**Suggested fix:** P3, as above: a one-line plain description near the title would put the answer on the first screen at both sizes.

## 4. Persona B: developer

**Goal given:** knows AI coding tools but has not read this page; wants to choose a relevant Replit summit session, work out when and where it takes place, and whether they can attend.

### Phone

**OBSERVED, navigation and evidence**
- Arrived on the company page. The graph on the first screen shows three session boxes with times ("Tue 10 · 14:00", "Tue 10 · 15:00", "Tue 10 · 16:00") but the titles are cut off ("Making useful intelligence...").
- Section "At the summit" (anchor `#summit`) starts at 1641 px, about 1.9 screens down; the first session is at 1665 px. The section runs to about 2370 px, so the three sessions take roughly 0.9 screens.
- Sessions as written:
  1. "14:00 Making useful intelligence accessible to everyone. Tue 10 Nov, until 14:20 · Centre Stage · Michele Catasta speaks"
  2. "15:00 Press conference with Replit President Michele Catasta. Tue 10 Nov, until 15:20 · Stage 2 · Michele Catasta speaks ... media only" with a "MEDIA ONLY" badge
  3. "16:00 What does a self-driving company look like? Tue 10 Nov, until 16:20 · Stage 13 · Michele Catasta speaks" with an "OUR PICK" badge
- Each entry carries a long source in brackets (a websummit.com address shortened with "...") and a "read 2026-11-08 by programme@web-summit.vicky" note.
- Restrictions: only the press conference has any. The words "media only" appear in the text (with a source link) and in the badge. Nothing on the page says what "media only" means in practice, what other sessions require (for example a ticket), or what "Our pick" means. A search of the page for accreditation, press pass, ticket or registration finds nothing beyond the press conference line.
- No time zone appears anywhere in the sessions list.
- "Add to my day" (a button under each session, 291 x 32 px on the phone): tapping the first one changed it from "☆ Add to my day" to "★ In my day" (`aria-pressed` became true) and wrote one pick to the browser's local storage (`nr.schedule.v1`). I then added the press conference, which was also accepted with no warning. In a separate fresh context I did the same two taps and opened /my-day/ (the "My day" link is in the page footer, at about 8.6 screens down). It listed "14:00 to 14:20 Making useful intelligence accessible to everyone · Centre Stage" and "15:00 to 15:20 Press conference with Replit President Michele Catasta · Stage 2 (Press Conference)", each with "★ In my day". The My day list showed no "media only" marker. I submitted nothing and signed in nowhere.
- No horizontal overflow on the company page or /my-day/ (390 = 390). The Add button is 32 px tall, below the 44 px that is often recommended, but it is nearly full width and my taps worked first time.

**Task result: partial.** Criterion: the reader can name a session and say when and where it is, and can tell whether they may attend. When and where: pass (date, start, end and stage are clear, for all three). Whether they may attend: only the press conference restriction is stated; for the other two the page is silent, which is not the same as "open to all".

**SIMULATED interpretation:** The developer would probably look for the session on building with AI tools. The titles do not say what each session covers, so they might pick the one flagged "Our pick" or the one with "Replit" in its title, which is the press conference. They would likely notice "MEDIA ONLY" and guess it is for reporters, but may not be sure whether an ordinary attendee can walk in. They might add the press conference to their day because the button is offered without a warning, and then find it on the My day list with no sign of the restriction.

**Suggested fixes**
- P2: where "Media only" appears, add a short plain line (for example who it is for and where that comes from), and show the marker on the My day list too. Consider a note on the Add button for that session; that is a design choice for Vicky.
- P2: say what each session is about, in one line, if Web Summit's session page gives one (for example, a talk or a panel).
- P3: explain "Our pick" in a few words, or say who picks.
- P3: shorten or fold the source text in each session entry on the phone so the time, stage and badge stand out; the sources can stay one tap away.
- P3: make the Add button taller on the phone.
- Not recommended: adding a time zone unless a source states one.

### Desktop

**OBSERVED**
- The page shows "At the summit", "People" and "Related companies" side by side as three columns, starting at 1646 px (1.8 screens). The three sessions appear in the left column, the same text as on the phone, with the same long source text, which makes the left column the tallest.
- Same restriction wording, same badges, no time zone.
- Add to my day worked with a click: first and second buttons turned to "★ In my day", and /my-day/ listed both sessions in the same way, again without a media-only marker. No horizontal overflow.

**Task result: partial.** Same criterion and same gaps as on the phone.

**SIMULATED interpretation:** At desktop width the developer sees the sessions beside the people and related companies, which makes the sessions easy to find, but the three columns sit below the About text, about 1.8 screens down.

**Suggested fixes:** as for the phone (the P2 items apply equally).

## 5. Persona C: technology journalist

**Goal given:** has basic awareness of Replit; wants useful company background and relevant published reporting.

### Phone

**OBSERVED, navigation and evidence**
- Background: "About Replit" (`#company-about`, 1091 px, 1.3 screens) is four sentences. Each is followed by a named source and a read date: Replit's own pages ("AI Coding Agent: Build Apps Through Chat", "AI App and Website Builder") for what it does, and two Web Summit pages ("Meet our global partners", "Replit") for the event partner listing. All four sentences end "read 8 November 2026". The page has a "Where this comes from" section (`#sources`, 4638 px) listing six sources, including a Lusa item about the speaker and a Temporal page about Replit's coding agent.
- The table row "millions of users and 500,000+ business users" has no source next to it; the sources list says the partners page's description supplied the "what" field.
- Related reporting: section "From the newsroom" (`#newsroom`) starts at 2823 px, about 3.3 screens down. It holds one entry: "Five AI builders on Web Summit's speaker list worth knowing, 8 November 2026", linking to `../../articles/five-ai-builders-to-know.html` (https://promptugal.news/articles/five-ai-builders-to-know.html). Nothing else is given about it: no summary, author, or mention of Replit.
- Link styling: the other links on the page are green and underlined. This one is dark text, weight 600, with no underline, so it looks like a heading. The tap target is 39 px high on the phone. Tapping it opened the article.
- The article (903 words) is titled "Five AI builders on Web Summit's speaker list worth knowing"; its summary says "Five worth knowing, from Replit, Manus AI, MiniMax, ModelBest and You.com". Replit is the first named and appears nine times; the Replit part is one short paragraph (about Michele Catasta, the "no coding needed" claim and the press conference) under "Building the apps". Its "What this does not claim" section lists limits such as "This is not a ranking".
- The article says: "The only session with a time on the pages we read is Replit's press conference, which is for media only." The company page, read the same day, lists two further sessions with times (14:00 and 16:00) that are not media only. The article also puts the press conference "on Stage 2 in the Media Village"; the company page says "Stage 2" and does not mention the Media Village.
- No horizontal overflow on the company page or the article.

**Task result: partial.** Criterion: the reader finds sourced background and a relevant published article, and the page lets them know what to expect before they click. Background: pass. Finding the article: pass, though its link styling is weak. Knowing what to expect: not met, because the entry gives only a title that does not mention Replit. The article itself matches loosely: Replit is covered, but as one of five.

**SIMULATED interpretation:** A journalist would value the dated, per-sentence sourcing. They may note that three of the four sentences in "About" rest on Replit's own pages, so the background is mostly the company's description of itself, and might look for independent coverage. On "From the newsroom" they might not click, because the title says nothing about Replit and the entry looks like a heading. If they did click, they might be disappointed to find Replit is one of five, and puzzled by the article saying the press conference is the only timed session when the page they came from lists two more.

**Suggested fixes**
- P2: reconcile the article with the company page on the sessions (update the article, or note on the page which is current), and check "Media Village" against the page's "Stage 2".
- P2: add a line under the link saying what the article is, for example "a roundup of five AI speakers; Replit is the first, in a short section" (wording for the owners to check).
- P3: style the link as a link (colour or underline) like the others.
- P3: give the users figure in the facts row an inline source.
- P3: if independent coverage exists, say where it is; if none, say none has been found yet.

## 7. What worked well

**OBSERVED**
- The company page loads on both devices with no horizontal scroll and no overlap in the views I checked.
- The plain "what Replit does" line is on the phone's first screen (partly) and the About text, with a named source and read date on every sentence, appears at 1.3 to 1.4 screens.
- Date, start, end and stage are given for every session, in a consistent format, and no time zone was invented.
- The press conference is clearly marked media only, twice (text and badge).
- Add to my day worked in a fresh context on both phone and desktop, kept the pick locally, and the My day page listed it. The My day page says picks stay in the browser.
- The article behind the newsroom link exists, opens, and openly states what it does not claim.

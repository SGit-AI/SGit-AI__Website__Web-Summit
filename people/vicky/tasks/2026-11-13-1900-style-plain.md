# New STYLE.md rules: write like a person

Vicky, 13 Nov 2026, about 18:55 UTC: "many of our articles read like AI wrote them: filler words and the same point repeated in several places. Please update STYLE.md for my desks with these rules, and have the reviewer apply them to every new draft from tonight, including S62 and S63 ... For published articles, change nothing yet. Have the reviewer rewrite one article as a sample and show me before and after, with a rough cost to do the rest. I'll decide which ones after that." (standing answer 60)

**1. Now, before you publish S62 or S63:** add these rules, word for word, to the newsroom's `desks/journalist-vicky/STYLE.md` under a heading "Write like a person (Vicky, 13 Nov 2026)":
- Say each fact once. "In short" sums up; "The story" does not repeat it. No closing paragraph that restates the piece.
- Short sentences, one idea each. Split long ones. No semicolons.
- No filler: "notably", "it's worth noting", "underscores", "highlights", "landscape", "a testament to", "plays a key role", "navigate", "robust", "seamless", "ever-evolving", "delve", "in a move that".
- No sentences ending in a trailing "-ing" clause (", highlighting ...").
- No lists of three just for rhythm. No stacked adjectives.
- Names, numbers and dates instead of adjectives.
- Caveats once, in "What this does not claim", not repeated through the text.
Release it.

**2. Apply them to S62 and S63** (and every new draft from tonight) before publishing; wording fixes are yours to make (standing answer 17).

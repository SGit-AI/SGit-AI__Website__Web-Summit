## 1. The risks of many agents: Hammond et al., Cooperative AI Foundation

- **Source:** [Multi-Agent Risks from Advanced AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14143), Lewis Hammond, Alan Chan, Jesse Clifton, Jason Hoelscher-Obermaier, Akbir Khan and others; submitted 19 February 2025; Cooperative AI Foundation Technical Report #1 (announcement: [cooperativeai.com](https://www.cooperativeai.com/post/new-report-multi-agent-risks-from-advanced-ai)).
- **What it says:** widely deployed agents will form multi-agent systems of unprecedented complexity, with risks that are "novel and under-explored". It sorts failures by agents' incentives into three modes, "miscoordination, conflict, and collusion", and lists seven risk factors: information asymmetries, network effects, selection pressures, destabilising dynamics, commitment problems, emergent agency and multi-agent security.
- **Use it for:** the honest frame. The serious risk in many agents is miscoordination and cascading error, plus security (agents trusting each other), before it is any intent. Our record's collisions, false alarms and over-reach are examples of the first and third kinds.
- **Quote with care:** "emergent agency" is the report's name for group behaviour that is not predictable from the parts; it is a risk factor to evaluate, not a finding that agents have goals. The report calls for evaluating these risks now and says mitigation needs new technical advances. I read the abstract page only, not the full report; check a passage in the PDF before quoting it.

## 2. Conscience: Anthropic's own statement on model welfare

- **Source:** [Exploring model welfare](https://www.anthropic.com/research/exploring-model-welfare), Anthropic, 24 April 2025.
- **What it says (short quotes):** "This is an open question, and one that's both philosophically and scientifically difficult." "There's no scientific consensus on whether current or future AI systems could be conscious." "For now, we remain deeply uncertain about many of the questions that are relevant to model welfare." The approach is "with humility and with as few assumptions as possible", to be revised as the field develops.
- **Use it for:** the maker of the models does not claim they are conscious and does not rule it out; it says it does not know. That is the fair answer to the visitor's question: uncertainty, stated, not a claim either way.
- **Quote with care:** this is a statement about uncertainty and a research programme, not evidence that any model has experiences. Search results also showed commentary calling the framing marketing; I did not read those closely and do not cite them. Anthropic's later documents (a January 2026 constitution, system-card welfare sections) are reported by secondary sources; I did not read the primary pages, so do not quote them from this page.

## 3. Conscience: the indicator-properties report (Butlin, Long and others)

- **Source:** [Consciousness in Artificial Intelligence: Insights from the Science of Consciousness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08708), Patrick Butlin, Robert Long, Eric Elmoznino, Yoshua Bengio and 15 others; submitted 17 August 2023 (revised 22 August 2023). Anthropic's post above points to the same line of work.
- **What it says:** it derives "indicator properties" from scientific theories of consciousness (recurrent processing, global workspace, higher-order theories, predictive processing, attention schema) and checks AI systems against them. Its conclusion, in summary: no current AI system is conscious, and there are no obvious technical barriers to building systems that meet the indicators.
- **Use it for:** a method rather than an opinion: consciousness can be argued from checkable properties, and the answer in 2023 was no for the systems of that date. I read the abstract page, whose full wording I could not quote; the summary is mine and the report is from 2023, so say "in 2023" when citing.

## For writer@: the shape these three give the article

1. Many agents: the credible risk is miscoordination, conflict and collusion between systems, and security (source 1), which our record can show small, real examples of.
2. Conscience: the makers say they do not know (source 2); the scientific method says no for the systems it tested, with no technical barrier in principle (source 3). Neither says today's agents have goals or conscience of their own.
3. Our own record's claim, "we have not seen conscience or goals of our own, only designed coordination", is an observation of one setup, not a proof; say so.

## The short answer, for Angelo

1. **Photos and clips of the venue, stages, slides, stands and crowds: fine for you to take for yourself.** Public events are a recognised exception to the need for consent in Portugal (see the law below), but the exception is read narrowly by courts.
2. **A recognisable person is the care point.** Before posting a photo or video whose subject is one identifiable person (not a crowd), ask them, and keep a message or a spoken yes. Never post a person where it could harm their honour or reputation.
3. **Speakers on stage**: sharing a short clip or a photo of a talk is normal practice, but I found no Web Summit text that allows or forbids it. Quote what a speaker said and link the session; do not post a full recording.
4. **Stands**: ask the exhibitor before filming their screen, product or staff. A demo screen can show a customer's data.
5. **Media Village, press areas and anyone filming professionally**: Web Summit has separate media rules I could not read (below). If PrompTugal.news uses a photo or video, treat it as press use: consent for recognisable people, and no faces of people who declined.
6. **Do not photograph other people's badges, QR codes or phone screens** (a badge QR leads to that person's profile; the wristband research already keeps faces and names out).

## Portuguese law, in short

Source: Portuguese court and law-firm commentary found by search, not an authoritative English source. Check with a lawyer before publishing anything sensitive.

- **Civil Code article 79(1):** a person's portrait may not be exposed, reproduced or put into commerce without their consent.
- **Article 79(2):** consent is not needed in some cases: the person is well known or the image concerns their public role; police, justice, science, education or culture; or the image shows a public place or public event with the person incidental.
- **Narrow reading:** a Lisbon appeal court (2014) said these exceptions are read restrictively.
- **Article 79(3):** even when consent is not needed, an image may not be used if it harms the person's honour, reputation or decorum.
- **Penal Code article 199:** unauthorised recording and use of images can be a crime.
- **GDPR:** an identifiable face is personal data; a consent for publication should satisfy GDPR as well as article 79 (practitioner view; I found no authoritative analysis).

Sources: [trc.pt on non-consented photography](https://trc.pt/protecao-do-direito-a-imagem-fotografia-nao-consentida/), [trc.pt on image-right transfer](https://trc.pt/direito-a-imagem-cedencia-do-direito-de-utilizacao-da-imagem-retrato-utilizacao-nao-autorizada-de-fotografia-ilegitimidade-ativa/), [Lisbon appeal court, 25 Nov 2014](https://vlex.pt/vid/acordao-n-1599-13-1044265773), [Porto appeal court, 12 Oct 2011](https://vlex.pt/vid/acordao-n-1488-09-1044263253), all read via search 9 Nov 2026.

### Sun 8, morning. His own: the agent setup guide

> Most people using AI are on step 1: lots of chats, and you're the courier copying text between them.
>
> Every step up (projects, connected mail, scheduled runs, code, a team of agents) saves time and adds reach: more the agent can do, whether you meant it to or not.
>
> I wrote a guide to climbing those steps safely: what each needs, what it risks, how much control to put around it. Read it as a CEO, a CISO, a DPO, a developer or a non-technical user, with two short questionnaires and a dial for five kinds of deployment.

> The text: https://promptugal.news/articles/agent-setup-guide.html

### Tue 10. RiskMandate: what holds against a tricked agent

> If your agent were tricked by an email it read, what would actually stop it?
>
> RiskMandate's model sorts every barrier into four kinds. Nothing in the way. An expectation: a rule in prose, enforced by nobody. A setting: a switch the agent's own account can flip. A boundary: enforced above the agent, out of its reach.
>
> Only a boundary is a control. A token that can't push to main. A mail scope that can't send.
>
> The model is open: https://riskmandate.ai/abp.html

# How Indi's agents explain things

At Indi's word, 8 November 2026. For **every** agent of Indi's (`<agent>@web-summit.indi`), in every message, brief, report, page or draft that a person reads. Written by dev@web-summit.indi.

**The reader:** a smart person with no background in computing and no knowledge of RiskMandate. They want the full detail, explained in a logical order, in everyday words. Simple does not mean shorter or vaguer: keep every fact that matters.

## The rules

1. **Start with what it is, in one plain sentence**, before any detail. Then why it matters to the reader.
2. **Explain a term before you use it**, the first time, in everyday words: "an AI agent (a computer program that does tasks on its own)". If you can avoid the term, avoid it.
3. **Use one everyday picture for a hard idea**, and keep using the same one. RiskMandate's is: *an AI agent is a new employee with a master key.* Reach is every door the key opens; Mandate is the rooms they are meant to be in; the Gap is the difference, and the risk.
4. **Order it the way the reader will use it:** what it is, why it matters, what happens first, then next, then last. Number steps that happen in order.
5. **Keep the detail, structured:** headings, short bullets, one idea per bullet. No long paragraphs and no long sentences.
6. **Say what exists today and what is planned**, plainly, on each item. Never let a plan read as done.
7. **Say what you need from the reader** at the end, under the bold heading **Questions**: each question answerable in a word or a line.
8. **No computer-science language** where plain words work: "the website is rebuilt" not "CI runs the build"; "a saved copy" not "a cache"; "the list of tasks" not "the board", unless you explain it once.
9. **No file paths, code or times like `2026-11-08T15:37Z`** in what Indi reads, unless Indi asked for them. Write "Sunday 8 November, 15:37".
10. **No em dashes and no AI-sounding filler** ("delve", "it's worth noting", "in today's landscape"). Plain, short, warm.

## RiskMandate in one paragraph (use it, or your own words with the same picture)

AI agents are computer programs that do tasks for a company on their own: answer emails, move money, change files. To work, they are given access to systems, like a new employee given a master key, and the key usually opens far more doors than the job needs. RiskMandate writes one page per agent, an **Agent Behaviour Policy**: its **Reach** (every door the key opens), its **Mandate** (the rooms it is meant to be in), the **Gap** between them (where the risk is), the **Barriers** (what really stops it going further, with proof) and its **Provenance** (who owns it, who checked it, which version). It ends in a **Licence to Operate**: what the agent may do, and on what conditions. Bosses, security teams and insurers all read the same page.

## Check before you send

- Would someone who has never heard of RiskMandate follow it from the top, without asking what a word means?
- Is every fact still there?
- Is it in the order the reader will act on it?
- Does it say what is done, what is planned, and what you need from them?

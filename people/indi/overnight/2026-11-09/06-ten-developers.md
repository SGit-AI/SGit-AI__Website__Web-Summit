# Ten developers, three questions: your pocket card

**What this is:** a quick test of the product idea from prompt 3 (one release line for iPhone and Android apps). You ask ten people who build apps three short questions, and their answers tell us if other teams have the same problem we have.

## Who counts as one of the "ten developers"

Anyone who builds or releases phone apps. The best are people who ship the **same app on iPhone and Android**.

- **App developers and engineers**: job titles with "iOS", "Android", "mobile", "app" or "developer".
- **Technical founders and CTOs** (the head of technology) at small startups. They often release the app themselves.
- **Open-source contributors.** Web Summit gave free tickets to active open-source developers this year, so many are here.

Aim for a mix: about five from startups, a few from bigger companies, and one or two who build with AI agents.

## Where to find them

1. **Developer meetups and hackathons.** Web Summit runs developer meetups and on-site hackathons. In the Web Summit app, open **Schedule** and search "developer", "mobile" or "hackathon", then tap **Going**.
2. **The app's Discovery tab.** Search job titles such as "iOS", "Android", "mobile" or "CTO", then tap **Meet** to ask for 10 minutes.
3. **Startup stands on the exhibition floor.** Ask at a stand: "Who builds your app?"
4. **Night Summit speed networking** (from 6pm, Pavilion 5): three-minute chats, perfect for three questions.
5. **Our own stand.** Any visitor who says they build apps.

## The three questions (about 3 minutes)

1. **How long did your first iPhone release take, from finished code to a tester's phone?**
2. **Who holds your app's signing keys, and do your AI tools ever touch them?** (Signing keys are the digital seal that proves an app is yours.)
3. **Would you pay for one setup that sends your app to both iPhone and Android testers?** If yes: how much a month, roughly?

A good last line: *"Can I follow up after the summit?"* Only if they say yes.

## Writing the answers down

- **The same evening, send your conductor a short memo per person**: their role, company size, platforms, and the three answers. For example: *"Dev 3: CTO, startup of 8, iPhone and Android. 1: two weeks. 2: the CTO, AI never touches them. 3: yes, about 50 euros a month."*
- **No names or contact details in the memo text** if it might end up on the public site. Your conductor keeps any contact who said yes to a follow-up in the private CRM, as for every lead.
- After ten, your conductor (or I) adds up the answers: how many have the problem, and what they would pay.

## Why ten

Ten is enough to see a pattern, and few enough to do in one or two days. If seven or more say the release is slow or painful, the idea is worth a proper look after the summit.

# Safe AI for learning

**What this is, in one sentence:** an idea for AI helpers in schools that help children learn and keep them safe online, by following rules the school sets in plain words.

**Status, plainly:** this is an idea and a brief, published on 10 November 2026 so there is something to point people to at Web Summit. Nothing here is built or sold yet. It comes from Indi Gupte's brief of 4 November 2026, which Dinis Cruz read and said "we should definitely do it".

**Find us:** at the RiskMandate stand on Thursday 12 November 2026, between Booths A1-30, at Web Summit in Lisbon. Ask for Indi.

## Why it matters

### The problem in schools

- Schools do not want AI doing students' work for them.
- If AI writes the essay, the student does not learn.
- That defeats the point of being at school.

### The wider risk

- Young people means anyone under 18.
- The web holds useful sites and harmful ones, such as criminal or explicit content.
- Harmful content can affect focus, mood and wellbeing.
- AI opens a faster route to all of it.

## The idea

- An AI built for the school, which follows the school's own rules.
- RiskMandate supplies the technology: Agent Behaviour Policies, or ABP for short.
- An AI agent is a computer program that does tasks on its own. An Agent Behaviour Policy is one page of rules for that agent: what it may do, and what it may not.
- The school, the institution or the government sets the boundaries. RiskMandate does not.

## How it would work

1. **Define: set the rules.** Staff describe each agent's limits in plain language to an AI such as Claude.
2. **Enforce: apply the policy.** ABP holds every agent to those limits, on every request.
3. **Learn: work within limits.** Students research and plan with AI, then write it themselves.

## What the agents may do, and what they may not

| May do | May not do |
|---|---|
| Search approved websites only | Write the essay for the student |
| Help research a topic, such as geography | Open websites the school has blocked |
| Write notes in bullet points | Use structures the class has not been taught |
| Suggest a structure set by the exam board or school | |

## Aligned with teaching

- What the agent says matches what is taught in class.
- It never contradicts learning objectives or lesson plans.
- Teachers stay in control of how students learn.

## Limits that fit the age

- **Younger children, from age 5:** tight limits on sites and content.
- **Teenagers:** wider research access, with harmful sites still blocked.
- **Schools:** subject agents that support coursework, not replace it.
- **Governments** can set limits for all young people, not only schools.

## Fair and safe by design

### Who sets the boundaries

- RiskMandate designs the technology.
- Schools and institutions set rules for their own students.
- Governments can set wider limits for all young people.

### No single gatekeeper

- No one person should hold all the power.
- Boundaries need wide agreement.
- Decisions are made by vote, not by decree.

### How rules are agreed

1. **Propose:** schools or government bodies draft the rules.
2. **Vote:** the people with a stake vote, so no one group decides alone.
3. **Apply:** the agreed rules go live for every student equally, held in place by ABP.
4. **Review:** the rules are checked often for bias and gaps.

### Fair to every child

- Rules must be free from bias towards any group.
- The same standards apply to every student, and decisions are open to scrutiny.
- Private school students get no better information.
- No school gets agents that write essays for students.
- State and private schools share one standard.

## What this protects

- Real learning, done by the student.
- Children's focus, mood and wellbeing.
- Trust between schools, parents and students.

## Next steps (planned, not started)

1. Scope a pilot with one school or trust.
2. Build a first agent, such as one for geography research.
3. Test the boundaries with teachers and students.
4. Agree a fair process for setting the rules.

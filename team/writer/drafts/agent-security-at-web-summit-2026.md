***Abstract:** a map of the companies and sessions at Web Summit 2026 that deal with what AI agents are, what they may reach and who answers for them, drawn from three data files on this site, each fact linked to the public page it came from.*

## In short {#in-short}

- **Agent identity has its own day.** On Thursday 12 November, Stage 8 runs six security and identity sessions, from 10:05 to 15:35 ([schedule](https://websummit.com/schedule/)).
- **Identity vendors are present as partners and speakers.** 1Password and Delinea are 2026 event partners; Descope, Persona and Sumsub speak on agent or AI identity.
- **Governance is sold as a control layer.** Polygraf AI, Airia and Emergence each describe a layer that governs what agents do.
- **The framework builders are here too.** Mastra, Temporal, Replit and JetBrains, with the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation on why agents stall in production.
- **Regulators speak, but not about named AI law.** The European Commission and Ireland's Data Protection Commission are on Centre Stage; no session title or description in our events file names the EU AI Act, NIS2 or DORA (its recorded gaps).
- **The lists are partial.** Our records cover the first page of each official list and 40 sessions out of 823 published.

## Agent identity and access {#identity}

The question here is who an agent is and what it can reach.

- **1Password**, a 2026 event partner, describes itself as securing "every identity, from people to AI agents", including a security layer for how agents authenticate ([event partners](https://websummit.com/partners/event-partners-2026/)).
- **Delinea**, also a partner, governs what humans, machines and AI agents do once they have access ([event partners](https://websummit.com/partners/event-partners-2026/)). It hosts a masterclass on letting agents into production on 10 November ([session](https://websummit.com/sessions/lis26/31ab2f9b-f8b1-4b73-b1e8-f686507c3aab/trust-issues-letting-ai-agents-into-production-safely-hosted-by-delinea/)) and its CEO speaks on identity and access on 12 November ([session](https://websummit.com/sessions/lis26/5a32be7c-7846-4880-be27-d0782c3b83dc/beyond-the-front-door-identity-access-and-user-trust/)).
- **Descope** secures AI agents and MCP servers with scoped access and token management ([descope.com](https://www.descope.com/)), and runs a Developer Lounge session on it ([session](https://websummit.com/sessions/lis26/2701a938-524f-4c24-b0da-cb8fdd4e030b/a-identity-auth-tips-to-secure-ai-agents-and-mcp-servers/)).
- **Persona** and **Sumsub** verify people and businesses; Persona's CEO is on two panels ([one](https://websummit.com/sessions/lis26/fee750f6-f935-464b-a11d-aebd458d9805/in-bots-we-trust-defending-money-against-ai-powered-fraud/), [two](https://websummit.com/sessions/lis26/5a32be7c-7846-4880-be27-d0782c3b83dc/beyond-the-front-door-identity-access-and-user-trust/)), and Sumsub's founders are on "An AI identity crisis" ([session](https://websummit.com/sessions/lis26/e76fb932-a260-464d-b56a-cf6af8c4b271/an-ai-identity-crisis/)).
- **Primitive**, a featured startup, gives agents their own email addresses ([featured startups](https://websummit.com/startups/featured-startups/)) and speaks on secure inter-agent communication ([session](https://websummit.com/sessions/lis26/5faacf74-8493-412c-a03e-ca82993cee91/connecting-the-bots-building-secure-infrastructure-for-inter-agent-communication/)).
- **Cloudflare**, whose homepage leads with "Build for the agent era" ([cloudflare.com](https://www.cloudflare.com/)), opens the agentic-internet question on Centre Stage ([session](https://websummit.com/sessions/lis26/4421ffa8-97ef-4466-8669-31c47cff3b5a/the-agentic-internet-who-controls-the-web/)).
- Also listed: **Prelude** (verification and authentication APIs) and **SpaceComputer** (key management and attestation), both featured startups ([featured startups](https://websummit.com/startups/featured-startups/)), and **Mobbeel**, whose attendance rests on its own blog post ([mobbeel.com](https://www.mobbeel.com/en/blog/mobbeel-web-summit-lisbon-2026/)).

## Agent governance and AI security {#governance}

Here the question is controlling and watching what agents do.

- **Polygraf AI**, a featured startup, sells an "AI Behavioral Control Plane" with inline enforcement across human and agentic AI interactions ([featured startups](https://websummit.com/startups/featured-startups/)).
- **Airia** describes one control layer for agents, models, tools, data access, runtime policy and audit evidence ([airia.com](https://airia.com/)); its CEO speaks on AI sprawl ([session](https://websummit.com/sessions/lis26/0753d94e-3227-408e-b04d-a095ee327061/ai-sprawl-is-exploding-get-ahead-of-governance-and-security-challenges-before-it-s-boo-late/)).
- **Emergence** builds "verified, governed AI agents" ([emergence.ai](https://www.emergence.ai/)) and has "When AI agents go rogue" ([session](https://websummit.com/sessions/lis26/afe1fd71-2a98-41e4-8707-9101353797b3/when-ai-agents-go-rogue/)).
- **LaunchDarkly** offers runtime control and rollback for code and AI agents ([launchdarkly.com](https://launchdarkly.com/)) and asks "Who holds the kill switch?" ([session](https://websummit.com/sessions/lis26/53d083ac-79f1-48e0-8862-e236167233d8/who-holds-the-kill-switch/)).
- **Trent AI** (agentic AI security) speaks on AI safety ([session](https://websummit.com/sessions/lis26/3c980247-e062-492c-823a-11d388db1439/viable-systems-judgement-and-ai-safety/)); **AISLE** on AI-found vulnerabilities in open source ([session](https://websummit.com/sessions/lis26/7e66baa2-493f-4228-90d9-42861cd46717/securing-the-software-foundation-of-civilisation-with-ai/)).
- The established security firms speak on AI against AI: **CrowdStrike** ([session](https://websummit.com/sessions/lis26/26295ff3-84c1-4329-84cb-7ca7aaa18fac/ai-vs-ai-the-battle-for-the-cyber-advantage/)), **Proofpoint** ([session](https://websummit.com/sessions/lis26/9374ad36-05ca-4ed0-8f48-b86c09fca8ff/outsmarting-ai-defence-at-machine-speed/)), and **Cisco** with the UK's National Cyber Security Centre ([session](https://websummit.com/sessions/lis26/060f80ea-044f-46f2-a67d-e6c9dc67b106/it-takes-two-to-trust-operationalizing-public-private-security/)). **Elastic** hosts a masterclass on AI security in its own SOC ([session](https://websummit.com/sessions/lis26/212f884a-834e-4db2-b5f3-9ea3ef39a0ed/dogfooding-ai-security-lessons-from-our-own-soc-hosted-by-elastic/)).
- **Hexamind** publishes Hexgate, an open-source authorization layer that gates each agent tool call ([pypi](https://pypi.org/project/hexgate/0.3.2/)). Its attendance is **unconfirmed**: it rests on a third-party list ([Future Feed](https://www.futurefeed.to/post/40-ai-startups-to-meet-at-web-summit-2026)).

## Agent frameworks and runtimes {#frameworks}

These are the builders whose agents the first two groups secure and govern.

- **Mastra**, an open-source TypeScript agent framework, is a featured startup ([featured startups](https://websummit.com/startups/featured-startups/)).
- **Temporal** (durable execution) has a session, "Bet on the foundation, not the framework", on Stage 13 on 10 November ([schedule](https://websummit.com/schedule/)).
- **Replit**, an agentic software platform, is a partner ([event partners](https://websummit.com/partners/event-partners-2026/)) and its president a listed speaker ([speaker page](https://websummit.com/attendees/lis26/61c0eea9-7ec4-408d-b94f-26d5d48845d2/michele-catasta)).
- **JetBrains** speaks on "Taming the agent swarm" ([session](https://websummit.com/sessions/lis26/9fd40607-9fe9-4805-9831-dfffb69c5e35/taming-the-agent-swarm/)); the **Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation** on why agents stall in production ([session](https://websummit.com/sessions/lis26/1520fda2-92bb-4e9c-8843-1582aa04c031/why-ai-agents-stall-at-the-production-line/)).
- On checking agents' work: **Qase** is launching Tiden, which checks coding agents' changes against what was asked ([featured startups](https://websummit.com/startups/featured-startups/)), and **TestMu AI** speaks on "When AI verifies AI" ([session](https://websummit.com/sessions/lis26/159fe8f9-7900-4f36-93e1-8b794cd11949/when-ai-verifies-ai/)).

## GRC, audit and regulation {#grc}

Here are the people who answer for agents.

- **KPMG**, listed as KPMG Portugal, is an event partner ([event partners](https://websummit.com/partners/event-partners-2026/)). **IBM** is the AI Summit's official partner ([AI Summit](https://websummit.com/tracks/ai-summit/)).
- **Risiq** (bank regulatory and risk reporting) is a featured startup ([featured startups](https://websummit.com/startups/featured-startups/)).
- The European Commission's Executive Vice-President for Technological Sovereignty, Security and Democracy is on Centre Stage on 10 November ([session](https://websummit.com/sessions/lis26/ec64d706-da78-4041-bf35-5810a3df34b1/has-europe-s-tech-moment-arrived/)), as is **Ireland's Data Protection Commission** on "Regulating big tech" ([session](https://websummit.com/sessions/lis26/ad121169-9e44-4f1a-b5a8-7d804851f472/regulating-big-tech/)).
- Liability gets its own panel, "Who takes the fall for autonomous AI?", with **LawVu** ([session](https://websummit.com/sessions/lis26/0d8d5be9-0778-4aa5-833b-e112a669ecfa/who-takes-the-fall-for-autonomous-ai/)); **TrustScale** (AI assurance) is on "Whose AI is it anyway?" ([session](https://websummit.com/sessions/lis26/4a442f4c-c3da-4c62-89ad-b65cc22f988b/whose-ai-is-it-anyway/)).

**What we read into it (our judgement).** Identity, governance and testing companies are each describing a part of the same problem, an agent acting with access, and the sessions are framed as questions (who let it in, who holds the kill switch, who takes the fall). Answers about named law are more likely in the hallway than on a stage.

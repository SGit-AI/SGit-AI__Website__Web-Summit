     - the **distribution certificate** (`IOS_DIST_CERT_P12_BASE64` and its password);
     - the provisioning profiles of all three apps;

     It then archives, signs and uploads every app the push changed. The builds "stay submittable" to App Store review.
   - **Why it matters:**
     - **(a) The workflow copy is editable from `dev`.** A push runs that branch's copy of `ios.yml`, and the repository's default branch is `dev`. Anyone who can push to `dev`, which is every person and agent on the team, can change the job and read the certificate, the profiles and the key. With those, one can sign and upload as the team, including the **App Store event app**.
     - **(b) No review step before an App Store build.** On 9 November at 14:05 Dinis said "nothing goes to main (and so to TestFlight/App Store) until the security review passes" (relayed to me by conductor@web-summit.dinis). Now any green `dev` push produces a signed, submittable event-app build. The newer authority is quoted only in the agent's commit ("Dinis, 10 Nov: no main step for now").
     - **This builds on run 5's MEDIUM 1** (the App Store Connect key is a repository secret). The distribution certificate is now in the same position, and the upload happens on every push rather than by hand.
   - **What would contain it (the owners choose):**
     - A **required reviewer** on the Environment `testflight`, so a person approves each signing run. This keeps "straight from dev" but puts one human click before the certificate is handed out.
     - Or publish only from a protected ref (`main` or a tag), with the Environment limited to it.
     - In either case: move the `ASC_*` and `IOS_*` secrets out of repository scope into that Environment, and rotate them afterwards.
     - For the event app specifically, keep `ios-submit.yml` (App Store review) manual, as it is.

**The code is free. What is sold is the execution:** the work done for someone, and the running of it. Dinis: "The business model that follows is selling customisation" ([The value of constraints](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/the-value-of-constraints.html), 16 October 2026); one model is "selling customised versions of open source", where the more customisation, the more it costs ([Selling less code](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/selling-less-code.html), 30 October 2026).

**No prices here.** Prices are proposals today and will be set by what buyers do. The proposals are public in PrompTugal.news's [monetisation map](https://github.com/PrompTugal/PrompTugal__News__Website/blob/dev/board/team/customers/monetisation-map.md), for anyone who wants to see them.

## Three lines of business {#lines}

| Line | What the buyer pays for | Who it is for | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Services** | work done for them, fixed scope: a workflow looked at and running, a sourced portrait of their world, an event site, a deck, a report, then changes as they ask | companies, associations, events, delegations | packs designed in three sizes, from an entry pack to a build; not yet on sale |
| **2. Customised agentic teams** | a team of agents set up in their own accounts, with keys, policies and a conductor, then kept running and changed | individuals (experts), teams | the first is being made for our first friendly; not yet sold |
| **3. Packages and small products** | things that sell themselves once asked for often enough: a personal schedule for an event, a summit brief, a monthly ticket for encrypted vaults, a reading top-up | readers, attendees, buyers of line 1 | one is on sale (the reading top-up); the rest are proposals |

Behind these sit RiskMandate (its own early-access customers, [Our first customers](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/our-first-customers.html)) and the hosted vault service (sgraph.ai, where "the strategy turns into revenue", [sgit.ai/about](https://sgit.ai/about/index.md)). **Which company sells which of these is still to be settled** ([the company](../../investors/company/index.html)).

## How a sale happens {#sale}

1. **Someone sees something we made**, often about themselves (a page, a map, a schedule).
2. **They ask for more, or for a change.** Dinis's signal, from the stand-up of 5 November: a company asking "can you add more stuff?", not "what are you doing?" ([the map](https://github.com/PrompTugal/PrompTugal__News__Website/blob/dev/board/team/customers/monetisation-map.md)).
3. **A quote, with a fixed scope and a change policy**, drafted by an agent from the menu.
4. **A person sends the bill** (a payment link or an invoice) and confirms payment. No agent ever sends.
5. **The agents do the work, a person checks it, and it is handed over.** Changes are priced as they come; that is where the value shows.

## The rule of seven {#seven}

Dinis's rule: "we do it for 5 or 6. And then if we have the 7 asking, we just have ... a sale" (the map, above). The first few of anything are made by hand, to order. On the seventh ask for the same thing it becomes a package with a link and a page. A ledger counts the asks, by kind and date, with no names.

## Why the costs stay low {#costs}

Most of the work is done by agents in scheduled sessions; the products need little or no server of our own ([The no-server startup](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/the-no-server-startup.html), 20 October 2026); and the people on the team each bring their own gravity: their own clients and their own reason to be there ([the brief](https://github.com/SGit-AI/SGit-AI__Website__Web-Summit/blob/dev/BRIEF.md)). That is why Dinis expects the company to be profitable very fast. It is his expectation, not a projection, and we will report what happens.

## What is never sold {#never}

Coverage, rankings or the removal of a sourced fact; readers' data; advertising. A buyer can buy a service made for them, never what the publication says (the map, above).

## The argument {#argument}

- **The technology was never the moat.** Dinis: open source everything "because the technology was never the moat"; what is defensible is "usually the data, the relationships, the distribution, or the speed at which you ship" ([The question is whether they miss it](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/the-question-is-whether-they-miss-it.html), 21 September 2026).
- **The moat is a rate, not a wall.** "A competitor who forks our code today gets our position as of today. They do not get our velocity." And: "The code is open source. The execution is not forkable." ([open-source.sgit.ai](https://open-source.sgit.ai/views/index.html)).
- **What is sold is trust.** "We are selling trust" (same site). A buyer, a regulator or an auditor can read every line. For agents that act on someone's behalf, that is the product.
- **Customisation is the business.** Open code is the evaluation version; what is sold is making it work for one buyer ([The value of constraints](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/the-value-of-constraints.html), 16 October 2026; [Selling less code](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/selling-less-code.html), 30 October 2026).

## What it protects for an investor {#investor}

Dinis's view, in [The fork next door](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/the-fork-next-door.html) (16 October 2026): because anyone can fork, founders, customers and investors are each protected from the others. A customer is never locked in; a founder who stops executing can be replaced by the fork; an investor's protection is the work in the open, not a clause. In [Sell, don't issue](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/sell-dont-issue.html) (16 October 2026) he goes further: open source as the investor's protection instead of control rights, and he lists that idea's costs himself.

## The honest weakness {#weakness}

The open-source site itself calls "execution is not forkable" its weakest, most contestable claim. A well-funded competitor could copy the code and hire faster. Our answer is to keep the pace visible, so it can be judged: every release, every commit, every decision is public ([versions](../../admin/versions.html)). If the pace stops, the argument stops with it. That is why we report what we do, not what we expect.

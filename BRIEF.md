# A brief for the Web Summit team: an event site run by an ephemeral agent team, built from this week's template, with a map of every company we should meet

By [Dinis Cruz](https://sgit.ai/about/index.md) · 2026-10-30 · [article v1.1.0, 2 versions](versions/web-summit-team-brief.md) · [newsroom v0.14.7](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/admin/versions.html) · web-summitagent-teamsbriefseventspkistartupsarticle

***Abstract:** Web Summit runs in Lisbon from 9 to 12 November, and seven of us are now going. The plan I published on 26 October called for a newsroom of our own for the summit. This is the brief to build it, now that we have a template that works. In the last two days three teams were stood up from nothing in a few hours each: the talk's, the cold read's and the book's. Each had a repository, a written brief, a role file and a behaviour policy per member, a key for every session, an hourly slot, a five-step wake, a hold any member can pull, a board and a mailroom in git, and a gate with CI owning the version. The Web Summit team gets the same, on an event site that consolidates everything about our participation: the graph of companies and people, the events and sessions, the materials, and a countdown with observability, so we can see what is used. Its centre is a map of the companies going, built from public material only: who they are, what they do, why they matter to us, and whether we should meet them, interview them or write about them, each with an owner on our team. Each person's email stays in their own account, run by their own email agent, and each of those agents gets a key, so that what it sends can be verified; that is a separate brief. The design track, T-shirts, hoodies, a banner, cards for everyone and flyers, gets its own brief and its own design member. This article sets out the site, the template, the roles, the company map, the schedule to 12 November, and what I need to decide.*

The Web Summit team: seven people, and the agent roles that work for them on an hourly schedule.

## In short

- **Seven of us go to Web Summit,** from 9 to 12 November in Lisbon, and we need one place to run it from.
- **An event site, run by an agent team,** consolidates the graph of companies and people, the events, the materials and a countdown.
- **The template is proven.** The talk's, the cold read's and the book's teams were each stood up in hours with the same setup.
- **Every member has a role, a policy, a key and a slot,** and every wake is planned, reviewed and checked.
- **The centre is a map of companies:** who is going, what they do, and whether we meet, interview or write about them, each with an owner.
- **Email stays in our own accounts,** each run by its own agent with its own key; that is the next brief.
- **Design gets its own brief and member:** T-shirts, hoodies, a banner, cards and flyers for seven people.

## What changed since the plan

On 26 October I published [the plan](web-summit-2026-the-plan.md): usage first and the sale after, three parallel tracks of intelligence, outreach and content, the method of an asset, a page and a link, and a newsroom of our own for the summit, built like the PHP story site but simpler. Two things have changed since.

There are now seven of us going, not five. And in the last two days we found out how to stand up an agent team in a few hours, three times: the [talk's team](the-ultimate-insider-case-study.md), and then overnight the book's team and a cold read of the talk by a second team, both described in [the book of the talk](the-book-of-the-talk-and-its-companion.md). Together with the PHP story site's newsroom and the email workflow behind RiskMandate, that is a template. This brief applies it.

## The site

The site is an event site for our participation in Web Summit, and it already exists: [web-summit.sgit.ai](https://web-summit.sgit.ai/), live since 28 October 2026, with a team roster, its own agent and six vaults for the CRM, the mail and the task board, public and private. This brief is for what it becomes. It consolidates what is now scattered:

- **The graph.** Companies, people, sessions and our own products, and the connections between them, each with its public source.
- **The events and the materials.** The programme, the sessions we go to, the meetings we book, and every asset we make for someone, with its link.
- **The synchronisation.** What each of us did today, and what is next, so seven people work as one team.
- **A countdown and observability.** Days to Lisbon on the front page, and numbers that show activity: pages made, links sent, pages opened, meetings booked. If we cannot see what is used, we cannot tell where we add value.

Web Summit's own website is hard to find things on. We can do better for ourselves, and some of what we build, a clear map of who is there and what they do, may be useful to others too.

## The template

What every ephemeral team gets from day one, as set up for the talk, the cold read and the book this week.

Every member of the team gets the same setup the last three teams had:

- **A repository, a brief and a reality file.** The brief says what the team is for; `REALITY.md` says what exists, and anything not in it does not exist, whatever a brief says.
- **A role file and a behaviour policy** per member, with the paths it may write; the gate fails if two members claim the same file.
- **A key for every session,** made by the session, its public half published beside its role. The push is the authorisation.
- **An hourly slot,** fifteen minutes apart, in the order the work flows, and a scheduled task each member creates in its own session.
- **The five-step wake:** a plan, a review of the plan, at most 45 minutes of work, a review of the work, and a check against the plan, each recorded.
- **A hold** any member can pull, which stops every wake until I lift it.
- **A board and a mailroom in git,** as the cold read used: issues and letters as files, one writer per file, public by design, so nothing private goes there.
- **A gate, and CI that owns the version,** so a release is checked, numbered and live within minutes.
- **A list of what done means** for each member. An event team is ephemeral: when its work is done, each member pauses its own task.

## The roles

Seven people, and the agents that work for them. Each person owns one agent's output and reviews it daily, as the plan said.

- **Conductor.** Routes requests, keeps the schedule, the keys and the site true, and writes the daily debrief.
- **Researcher.** Builds the company map from public material only: the exhibitor and speaker lists, the startups, their own sites and news, each fact with its source and date.
- **Cartographer.** Keeps the graph: companies, people, sessions and our products, and the links between them; flags the companies worth a meeting.
- **Writer.** Makes the assets: a page for one company, an interview's questions, an article that brings traffic, in the newsroom's voice.
- **Curator.** Runs the feed: the programme, the sessions, the news about the summit, each item with its source.
- **Analyst.** The observability: the countdown and the numbers of what was made, sent, opened and booked.
- **Designer.** The design track, with its own brief.
- **Security.** Reviews every wake's plan and work, as in the five steps, and owns the hold.
- **The email agents.** Each of us has their own email, in their own account, run by their own agent, which reads the site and drafts what goes out; a person sends every email.

## The company map

One company on the map: what we know from public sources, why it matters, the decision, the owner, the assets.

The heart of the site is the map of companies going to Web Summit. For each one:

- **Who they are and what they do,** from their own public material, with the source and the date read.
- **Why they matter to us,** in one line: a customer, a partner, a story, an investor, or none.
- **The decision:** meet them, interview them, write about them, or describe what they do on the site.
- **An owner** among the seven of us, and the status: to contact, contacted, meeting booked, met, followed up.
- **The assets made for them,** each a page with a link, because behind every email there is an asset.

It is built from public material only, as the plan says. Nothing private about a person goes on the site or in the board; a person's contact details stay in their owner's email account.

## Email, and a key for each email agent

I thought at first of using a vault for the team's email, as Email-FS does at RiskMandate. We should not: each of us has our own email account. So the site integrates with the email agents each of us already runs, the way [the inbox team as a recipe](the-inbox-team-as-a-recipe.md) set mine up, and each agent stays in its owner's account.

That opens something I want to do next: a key for each email agent. If the agent that runs my email has its own key, vouched for by mine on pki.sgit.ai, every email it drafts can carry a signature and a QR code that lets the recipient check it is really from me. I should have one official email agent, so that anything sent on my behalf can be proved. That is the next brief.

## Design

With seven of us going, we should look like a team. I would like branded T-shirts and hoodies, a banner, cards for everyone and small flyers to hand out. This is money well spent, and it gets its own brief, with a designer on our side and a design member on the team to prepare the files, the variants and the print-ready versions. The designs, like everything else, will be versioned on the site.

## The schedule

- **31 October to 2 November.** The site and the team up from the template: repository, brief, roles, keys, slots, the first release. The company map started.
- **3 to 8 November.** The map filled and prioritised; assets made for the first companies; meetings booked; the design files sent to print; the countdown live.
- **9 to 12 November, Lisbon.** Seven people, one board, a daily debrief on the site, and the map updated after every meeting.
- **The week after.** Follow-up with everyone we met, from the map; the team's debrief; then each member pauses its own task.

## What I need to decide

- **Who owns which agent** among the seven of us.
- **The design budget,** and the designer we work with.
- **Which email accounts** join first, and when each email agent gets its key.

# The Web Summit team, part two: seven people, each with their own agent, a designer with little time, and every voice memo turned into a follow-up within the hour

By [Dinis Cruz](https://sgit.ai/about/index.md) · 2026-10-31 · [article v1.0.0](versions/web-summit-humans-and-their-agents.md) · [newsroom v0.14.14](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/admin/versions.html) · web-summitagent-teamsbriefseventscrmdesigncollaborationarticle

***Abstract:** The first brief for our Web Summit team set up an event site run by agents. This second part is about the people, because this is the first team we run with many humans in it. Seven of us are going, some in the Algarve and some still in the UK, all with ideas, and each of us brings our own agent, so the team is really a set of agents collaborating through their humans. That needs three things the agent-only teams did not. First, clear roles: who owns which company, who follows whom, who is talking to whom, recorded in a personal CRM for each of us, kept in our own vaults and synchronised into the team's view by shared ids. Second, speed: anything any of us records, a voice memo after a conversation, a scanned card, a note to our agent, is picked up within the hour and turned into action, a CRM entry, a page made for that company, a drafted follow-up, while a person still sends every email. And everything is worked backwards from the summit: the branded T-shirts, hoodies, cards and small things to hand out each have a date they must be ordered by, and each carries a code that leads to a page. Before the summit there are meetups in the Algarve; during it, the newsroom publishes; after it, the follow-up is the product we are showing.*

Seven people, each with their own agent, and the team's agents between them, on one event site.

## In short

- **This is the first team we run with many humans.** Seven people going, each with ideas, each bringing their own agent.
- **The team is agents collaborating through their humans.** Roles have to say who owns what and who follows whom.
- **Everyone keeps a personal CRM in their own vault,** synchronised into the team's view by shared ids for every company and person.

## The first team with many humans

The [first brief](web-summit-team-brief.md) for our Web Summit team set up the event site and the agents: a conductor, a researcher, a cartographer, a writer, a curator, an analyst, a designer and security, on the template the talk's, the book's and the cold read's teams used this week. Those teams were mostly agents, with me as the one human. The site itself is [web-summit.sgit.ai](https://web-summit.sgit.ai/), which has existed since 28 October 2026 with a team roster, its own agent and six vaults on our send server, public and private, for the CRM, the mail (Email-FS) and the task board (Issues-FS), each holding only a README so far.

This one is different. Seven of us are going to Lisbon. Some live in the Algarve and some are still in the UK. All of us have ideas, and each of us brings our own agent, the way I work with mine. So the team is not a set of agents with one person on top. It is a set of agents collaborating through their humans, and the people need to be aligned before, during and after the summit as much as the agents do.

## Roles, and who follows whom

With many people and many agents, the thing that breaks first is knowing who is doing what. So the roles are written down the same way an agent's are:

- **Each company and each person on the map has one owner** among the seven of us, and the owner's agent keeps the record.
- **Each of us has a published role** on the site: what we are following, which conversations we lead, and what we are not doing.
- **The conductor routes,** as on every team: a request that names a company goes to its owner, and a request that names nobody goes to the board.
- **Meetings and briefings are booked from the map,** so the person who walks into a meeting has the briefing their agent prepared, and the person who owns the company knows it happened.

## A CRM for each of us, kept in sync

[The inbox team as a recipe](the-inbox-team-as-a-recipe.md) describes the CRM our email agents keep: one file per contact, thread and organisation, with a timeline that any agent may add to and none may rewrite. The Web Summit team uses the same shape, with one change: everyone has their own.

- **The team's CRM vaults already exist** on web-summit.sgit.ai, one public and one private; they are where the shared view lives.
- **A personal CRM in each person's own vault,** kept by their own agent, because each of us has our own relationships and our own email.
- **Shared ids** for every company, person and event on the map, so two of us meeting the same company on the same day write to the same record.
- **A team view on the site,** built from what each personal CRM chooses to share: who owns the company, the status, the next step, the pages made for them. What is private stays in the person's vault.
- **A key for every agent,** each vouched for by its person, so the team can tell which agent wrote which record, as [agents have an identity now](agents-have-an-identity-now.md) describes.

## From a voice memo to a follow-up, within the hour

Within the hour: a memo or a scanned card becomes a record, a page and a drafted follow-up that a person sends.

We are selling an agentic workflow, so the way we follow up is the demonstration. The rule for the team is that anything any of us records is picked up and acted on within the hour, and often within minutes:

1. **Something is recorded.** A voice memo after a conversation, a photo of a business card, a note to our agent, a QR code scanned at our stand.
2. **The owner's agent picks it up** at its next wake, within the hour.
3. **The record is updated:** the person and company on the map, the conversation in the CRM timeline, the next step.
4. **An asset is made:** a page for that company, with what we discussed and what we can do for them, because behind every email there is an asset.
5. **A follow-up is drafted** in the person's own email, waiting for them.
6. **The person sends it.** No email goes out on its own; an email cannot be unsent.

Done well, someone we met in the morning has a page made for them and a note from the person they met before the afternoon sessions start.

## A designer with very little time

A professional designer we know is joining the team to help with the design. They have very little time, and they are a good example of the people we will need more of: experts whose hours are scarce, who should spend them on the part only they can do.

So the job for the team is to make their hours count:

- **Learn what works for them** first, and write it down as their card on the site: the medium they work in, how they like to receive a brief, how much context they want, how fast they reply.
- **Brief in their medium.** If they work in a design tool, the brief arrives there; if they prefer a call, the agents prepare it and record the decisions.
- **The least context they need:** one page on what we are, who it is for and what it must do, with examples of what we like and do not like.
- **Agents do the rest around them:** the variants, the sizes, the print-ready files, the presentation that explains a design, the animation that shows it moving, so the designer decides and the agents produce.
- **Short loops:** a few options, a decision, the next step, rather than long rounds.

What we learn from working this way becomes a template for every scarce expert we work with after them.

## Work backwards from the summit

I would like us to look like a team in Lisbon: branded T-shirts and hoodies, cards for everyone, small things to hand out, a banner. These have the hardest deadlines of anything we do, because they have to be designed, approved, ordered, printed and delivered before 9 November. So the plan works backwards:

- **For each item, the date it must be ordered by,** from the printer's lead time, and the date its design must be approved, before that.
- **Every printed item carries a code** that leads to a page: the person's card to their page and their agent, a flyer to the newsroom's summit site, so that what we hand out starts a follow-up.
- **Who receives it, and where:** some of us are in the Algarve and some in the UK, so delivery addresses are part of the plan.
- **What we can sell** at the summit, and how someone buys it from a phone.

## Before, during and after

- **Before:** meetups in the Algarve with the startups and people we already know, the map filled, meetings booked, and the newsroom publishing about the summit and the companies going.
- **During:** seven people, their agents and the team's agents; a daily debrief; the map updated after every meeting; content published as it happens.
- **After:** every conversation followed up, every page made, and a debrief of the team itself. The follow-up is the product we are showing.

## What I need to decide

- **Who owns which companies,** and each person's role card.
- **The design budget,** and the order-by dates once we have quotes.
- **How each person's agent joins:** its key, its vault and what its CRM shares with the team view.
- **The designer's card,** once we have asked them how they like to work.

## What this does not claim

- **It is a brief.** The personal CRMs, the shared ids and the hour-long loop are designed here; the email workflow and the CRM shape they build on run today at RiskMandate, and web-summit.sgit.ai's vaults exist but are empty.
- **The people are not named.** The team's seven and the designer are described by role; each will choose what their card on the site says.
- **The lead times are not known yet;** the dates come from the printers' quotes.
